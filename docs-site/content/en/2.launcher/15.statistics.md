**Statistics** shows how much you play – across all your instances or for just one. Every time you start an instance from Cubiq Launcher, the launcher notes a session: when you started and stopped, the instance with its Minecraft version and mod loader, and the servers you joined. The page turns that into totals, charts, a calendar and lists of your favourites.

## Open the statistics

- **Statistics** in the left sidebar, under **Collection**.
- Search with :kbd{value="Ctrl"} :kbd{value="K"} – "statistics", "play time" or "streak" all find the page.
- **All statistics** in the **History** tab of an instance opens the page for just that instance.
- In the app on your phone: **More → Statistics**.

![The Statistics page with six key numbers and a bar chart of the play time per day](/shots/launcher/play-statistics.png)

While a game is running, **Playing now** appears next to the title of the **Play time** chart and the numbers count up every minute. When you close the game, its session shows up right away. The round arrow button at the top right (**Refresh**) reloads the page. Haven't played yet? Then the page says **No play time yet** – **Open library** takes you to your instances.

::tip
Your statistics stay on your PC: friends can't see them, and they aren't synced to your other PCs.
::

## Key numbers

Six tiles at the top give you the overview:

| Tile | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Total play time** | All your play time. Below it: since when you've been playing – or "incl. … from before" if it contains earlier play time without a date (see [Your earlier play time](#your-earlier-play-time)). |
| **This week** | Play time since the start of the week, and below it "Today: …". |
| **This month** | Play time since the first day of the month. |
| **Sessions** | How many times you played, with the average length ("avg. … per session"). |
| **Longest session** | Your longest session, with its date and instance. |
| **Current streak** | How many days in a row you've played – and your record ("Best: …"). |

How the launcher counts:

- A **session** runs from the start of the game until it closes. Sessions shorter than a minute – for example when the game crashes while loading – don't count as sessions, but their time does.
- A day counts as **played** from one minute of play time. The streak and the calendar go by that.
- Your **current streak** runs up to today – or up to yesterday as long as you haven't played today yet. So it doesn't drop to zero in the morning; it only ends when a whole day passes without playing.
- Days begin at midnight on your PC's clock. A session that goes past midnight is split between the two days.
- Weeks start on the day that's usual for the launcher's language: Sunday in English, Monday in German and Spanish.

## Play time per day or week

The **Play time** chart shows one bar per day for the last 30 days. Click **16 weeks** at the top right for one bar per week, and **30 days** to go back. Above the bars you see the total for the shown time range; point at a bar to see its date and exact play time.

## Calendar

![The calendar of your gaming days, with the most played instances and Minecraft versions below](/shots/launcher/play-statistics-calendar.png)

The **Calendar** shows the last months at a glance: every square is a day, every column a week. The longer you played on a day, the fuller its green – from a soft shade to full green, as the scale **Less … More** underneath shows. The shades compare your days with each other: the quarter of days on which you played the most gets the full green. Next to the title you see on how many days you've played in total.

The calendar shows as many weeks as fit into the window – from about three months up to a whole year.

## Keyboard and table

The chart and the calendar both work with the keyboard. Press :kbd{value="Tab"} until one of them has a frame – the selection starts at today (or the current week):

| Key | Play time chart | Calendar |
|---|---|---|
| :kbd{value="←"} / :kbd{value="→"} | One day (or week) back / forward | One week back / forward |
| :kbd{value="↑"} / :kbd{value="↓"} | – | One day back / forward |
| :kbd{value="Home"} / :kbd{value="End"} | First / last bar | First day / today |

The selected day and its play time appear above it on the right.

**Show as table** under the chart and under the calendar lists the same numbers as text, newest first: for the chart every day (or week) of the range, for the calendar every day in it on which you played. **Hide table** folds it away again. Screen readers also get a short summary of each chart.

## Your favourites

Below the calendar the page ranks what you play most. Every row shows the play time, a thin bar compared to first place, the share in percent and the number of sessions. You see the top five; **Show all (…)** opens the rest.

- **Most played instances** – your instances by play time. Deleted instances keep counting: they keep their last name, are greyed out and marked "(deleted)".
- **Minecraft versions** – the versions you play most. The line under the heading shows your mod loaders with their share, for example "Fabric 59 % · NeoForge 15 % · Forge 12 % · Vanilla 9 %".
- **Servers** – the servers you joined, whether with **Join** in the launcher or from the multiplayer menu in the game. If you visit several servers in one session, its time is shared between them. The list appears as soon as you've joined a server.

## Just one instance

Pick an instance in the list at the top right (**All instances** shows everything again). Then every number, chart and list on the page applies to that instance alone – the line under the title says "Play time of …", and the list of instances disappears. Deleted instances sit at the end of the list, marked "(deleted)". The list appears once at least two instances have play time.

## On every instance page

The **History** tab of an instance starts with a summary of its play time: the total, how much of it was in the last 30 days, **Sessions**, **Average** and **Longest session**, and small bars for the last 30 days – point at a bar for its date and play time. Below that you see how often the instance crashed (if it did) and which version you mostly played it on ("Mostly on …"). **All statistics** opens the Statistics page for just this instance. The summary appears as soon as the instance has play time.

::note
The summary follows **Settings → Behavior → Show play time**. If you switch it off, the launcher hides the total play time on the library cards, the home page and the instance pages – and this summary too. The Statistics page itself stays. If you hide the History tab under **Settings → Features**, the summary goes with it.
::

## Your earlier play time

Nothing gets lost when the statistics start:

- The play time you already had counts from the start. Sessions from the history of your instances come in with their date and the Minecraft version you played back then.
- Older play time that the history doesn't cover counts towards the totals and your instances, but has no date – so it doesn't show up in the chart, the calendar or the streaks. The **Total play time** tile then says "incl. … from before", and the bottom of the page shows since when sessions are recorded.

## When the launcher closes while you play

The launcher notes a session as soon as the game starts. If the launcher closes unexpectedly while you play – it crashes or your PC switches off – its next start ends the session at the moment the game last wrote to its log, and adds that time to the instance's play time as well. If the game is still running when the launcher starts again, the session simply goes on until you close the game.

## Reset the statistics

**Reset statistics** at the bottom of the page lets you start over: after you confirm with **Reset**, all recorded sessions disappear from the statistics – charts, calendar, streaks and lists start again from zero. The play time shown on your instances stays, and recording starts again right away (a game that's running keeps counting).

::warning
Resetting can't be undone – earlier sessions don't come back, and your earlier play time isn't taken over again.
::
