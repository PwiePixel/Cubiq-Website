Cubiq Launcher backs up your singleplayer worlds by itself. When you press **Play** and after you close the game, every world that changed is saved as a ZIP file. A creeper blew up your base, a mod damaged your world or you simply regret a build? Go back to an earlier state with a few clicks.

## How automatic backups work

- **When you press Play**, the launcher backs up every world of the instance that changed since its last backup. The worlds you played last come first.
- **After you close the game**, it backs up the worlds you just played. Now nothing writes to them anymore, so the next start usually has nothing left to do.
- **Unchanged worlds are skipped.** A world you didn't play gets no new backup – most of the time there is nothing to do at all.

A backup contains the world itself – not the mods, resource packs or settings of the instance.

### The game never waits

The backup runs in the background while Minecraft loads. You can follow it in the **tasks panel** in the title bar, which shows the world being saved and how far along it is, and on the world card in the **Worlds** tab ("Backing up … 42 %"). If you cancel it in the tasks panel, the game keeps starting. When everything goes well, the launcher doesn't bother you with a message.

### Never from a half-saved world

Minecraft only touches a world when you open it in the game – after the game has loaded and you picked the world. Small and medium worlds are backed up long before that.

If you are faster and open a world before its backup is finished, the launcher notices and throws that unfinished backup away: a backup is never made from a half-saved world. Your previous backup stays, a short notice tells you what happened, and after you play, a fresh backup follows.

### When a backup fails

You get a short notice with the reason, for example "Backup of “My world” failed: …", and the game starts anyway – a backup problem never keeps you from playing. The launcher tries again after you play or at the next start.

## See your backups

Open an instance and switch to the **Worlds** tab. Every world card has a line with a clock:

- "5 backups · last 3 hours ago" – how many backups the world has and when the last one was made,
- "No backup yet" – there is no backup of this world so far,
- "Backing up … 42 %" – an automatic backup of this world is running right now.

![The Worlds tab: every world shows its number of backups and the last one, below them a deleted world with backups](/shots/launcher/world-backups-worlds.png)

Click the line with the clock to open **Backups of …**: all backups of that world, newest first, with date, size and the Minecraft version the world was last saved with. A label shows where each backup comes from:

| Label | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| **Automatic** | Made when you started the game or after you played. Older ones are removed by themselves – see [Settings](#settings). |
| **Manual** | Made with **Back up now**. Stays until you delete it. |
| **Before restoring** | The state of your world right before you replaced it with a backup. Stays until you delete it. |
| **Older backup** | A ZIP backup made in the Worlds tab of an earlier launcher version. You can restore it like any other. |

At the top of the window, **Open folder** shows the backup files of this world and **Back up now** makes a new backup. The trash can next to a backup deletes it after a short question ("Delete this backup?") – for good, it doesn't go to the recycle bin.

![The backups of a world with date, size, Minecraft version and kind](/shots/launcher/world-backups-list.png)

## Restore a world

::steps{level="3"}
### Close the game
A world can only be restored while its instance isn't running. As long as the game runs, **Restore** is greyed out.

### Pick a backup
In the **Worlds** tab, click the line with the clock of the world, then **Restore** next to the backup you want.

### Choose how
- **As a copy** adds the backup as a new world next to your current one, named like "My world (restored 2026-10-27 14.05)". Your current world stays as it is.
- **Replace the current world** puts the backup in place of your world. Before that, the launcher saves the current state as a backup (**Before restoring**), so you can always go back.

### Confirm
Click **Restore**. The tasks panel shows the progress; when it's done, the world is ready to play.
::

![Restoring a backup as a copy or in place of the current world](/shots/launcher/world-backups-restore.png)

::tip
Not sure which backup is the right one? Restore it **As a copy** first and look around in the game. If it's the one you want, keep playing in the copy – or restore the same backup again with **Replace the current world**.
::

### Bring back a deleted world

When you delete a world in the **Worlds** tab, it goes to the recycle bin (the trash on Linux) – its backups stay. Worlds that are gone but still have backups appear under **Deleted worlds with backups** below your worlds, with the number of backups and when the last one was made. Click **Restore**, pick a backup and choose **Bring the world back** – the world is back in your world list.

## Back up now

You can also make a backup by hand – for example before a risky build, before you try a new mod or before an update:

- the box button on the right of the world card (between the folder and the trash can), or
- **Back up now** at the top of the backups window.

This works even while the game runs, as long as that world isn't open in the game – otherwise the launcher asks you to close the world first. Manual backups also work when automatic backups are off, and they are never deleted automatically.

::warning
Switching an instance to a newer Minecraft version? Minecraft converts the worlds on the first start, and the old version can't open them anymore. Press **Back up now** first – the backup keeps the state from before the conversion. More in [Change the version](/en/launcher/instances#change-the-version).
::

## Settings

Open **Settings** at the bottom of the left sidebar and pick **World backups** in the **Instances** group – or jump there with :kbd{value="Ctrl"} :kbd{value="K"}.

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Automatic backups** | Backs up changed worlds when you start and after you play, for all instances. On by default. |
| **Keep per world** | How many automatic backups each world keeps: 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, 30 or 50 – 5 by default. Older automatic backups are deleted. |
| **Size limit** | A limit for all backups together, from 5 GB to 500 GB, or **No limit** (the default). When the backups get bigger, the oldest automatic ones go first. |

::note
Never deleted automatically: backups made with **Back up now**, the ones made **Before restoring** and older backups from earlier launcher versions. The newest backup of each world always stays, even with a size limit. You can still delete any backup yourself in the backups window.
::

### On or off for one instance

At the top right of the **Worlds** tab, **Auto backup** decides for this instance alone:

- **Default (on)** or **Default (off)** – follows the setting above,
- **On** – back up this instance automatically, even if automatic backups are off in the settings,
- **Off** – no automatic backups for this instance; **Back up now** still works.

Point at it to see what applies right now, for example "Automatic backups are on: changed worlds are saved when you start and after you play, the last 5 per world are kept."

### Storage

Further down in the same section, under **Storage**, you see how much space the backups take, for example "Space used: 1.4 GB · Backups: 12 · Worlds: 3". The backups are stored as ZIP files in the `world-backups` folder of the launcher's data folder – one folder per instance, with one folder per world inside. **Open folder** takes you there.

Deleting an instance doesn't delete its world backups. If there are backups of instances you removed, the launcher shows how much space they take ("… belong to deleted instances."). **Delete those** and then **Delete for good** frees that space.

**Older backups** from earlier launcher versions are the exception: they lie in the instance's own folder, so they go when you delete the instance. They also don't count here, in the number on the world card or under **Deleted worlds with backups** – you only see them in the backups window of their world.

::tip
Your backups usually sit on the same drive as your worlds. For really important worlds, copy a backup to a USB stick or a cloud folder now and then – every backup is a normal ZIP file.
::

## Good to know

- **Shared worlds:** if an instance shares its worlds with other instances (instance settings → **Shared folders** → **Worlds**), the backups belong to all of them, and the backup after playing waits until the last of these instances is closed.
- **Phones and tablets:** automatic backups are made by the launcher on Windows and Linux. The apps for Android and iPhone/iPad don't back up worlds by themselves – use **Back up now** there.
- **Worlds tab hidden?** If you hid it under **Settings → Features**, the automatic backups keep running. Show the tab again to see or restore them.
